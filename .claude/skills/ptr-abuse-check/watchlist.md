# Watchlist

A match here means **"proof of control required"**, never "reject". The decision
always belongs to the operator; an A record pointing at the requested IP clears a
watchlisted domain.

Match on the **registrable domain (eTLD+1)** of the requested hostname, plus its
obvious ccTLD variants (`dhl.com.tr`, `dhl.de`, …) and look-alike spellings.

## Look-alike patterns (no fixed list — evaluate)

- Character substitution: `l` ↔ `I` ↔ `1`, `o` ↔ `0`, `rn` ↔ `m`
- Punycode / IDN: any label starting with `xn--`
- Brand + affix: `-tracking`, `-cargo`, `-secure`, `-login`, `-support`,
  `-billing`, `-verify`, `-account`, `-update`, `-service`
- Brand on an unexpected TLD: `dhl.info`, `paypal.online`, `apple.shop`
- Phishing vocabulary in any label together with a brand name

## Invalid PTR targets (refuse regardless of who asks)

Listing criteria for Spamhaus CSS and rejected outright by many receivers:

- `localhost` · `localhost.localdomain`
- a bare IP literal (`1.2.3.4`) or an IP-shaped label
- default machine names: `WIN-XXXXXXX`, `ubuntu`, `srv`, `mail` with no domain
- single-label / unqualified names — a PTR target must be an FQDN
- malformed names: empty labels (`bad..hostname`), trailing junk, `_` in a
  hostname label

## Infrastructure signals (structure, not names)

- **Fast-flux**: the hostname resolves to many addresses across unrelated ASNs
  or countries. Overrides a passing A-record check — see step 4a of the skill.
- **Off-domain CNAME**: a hostname under a trusted brand pointing to a different
  registrable domain.
- **Fresh registration**: whois creation date under ~90 days on a brand-adjacent
  name.
- **PTR churn**: the same IP renamed repeatedly in a short window, or a customer
  cycling names across a block of IPs — the snowshoe profile. Check
  `query-ip-usage-logs-tool`; a previous PTR that was itself a third-party
  institution (e.g. an IP previously named under a `.edu` domain, now requested
  under a global brand) means the customer is a repeat offender, not a one-off
  mistake.
